# Evidence: RK3566 capacity (2026-11-18, HA Green)

The audit record for the budgets and the camera claim in
`boards/cairn_rockchip/priv/profiles/rk3566-tier1.yml`. Produced by
`tools/board-pipeline/rk-ladder.sh` over `rk-cell.sh`; the cells' logs stay
on the board and this file carries the verdicts and the identities.

## Setup

HA Green (RK3566, 4 × A55, 4 GB), Home Assistant Core and the Matter server
running throughout. Each cell is one `podman run` of the Rockchip release
image, `Cairn.BoardPipeline` compiled into the release's own VM by
`bin/cairn eval`: the shipped NIFs, `decoder: rkmpp`, the `rknn` backend
(rknn api 2.3.2, driver 0.9.8, librga 1.10.5), one engine. Every camera
plays one looped substream clip; the motion gate is open; `sample_fps 2`.

The three tool hashes are of the files as they ran; the copies in this
repository differ by review fixes made afterwards, none of which changes
what a cell that ran measures. The harness now fails a run with no
observations and refuses an env value that is not an integer or more engines
than streams. `rk-cell.sh` checks its numeric env, repeats its stop when
interrupted before the container exists, ends its sampler with the cell and
prints every engine's line. `rk-ladder.sh` takes one lock whatever its name,
validates `REF_OBS`, counts a `min_obs` of 0 as a miss and keeps an
interrupted cell's output.

Governors as found and never changed: cpu `schedutil`, NPU `rknpu_ondemand`
(the NPU read 900 MHz in every sample of the 70 cells whose sampler read
it; the first cell's did not). All 71 cells exited 0 with no `[error]` line.

## Reading a cell

- **Clean is 552 observations per camera in 300 s.** Every camera in every
  300 s cell below its rung's boundary read 552, at every rung, bar one
  that read 553. A camera under it is being starved by the engine. In
  120 s it is 221: the one 120 s cell that kept up read 221–222.
- A budget is the largest camera count clean in both 300 s repeats, times
  the 1.875 passes/s floor rate the loader divides by. It is never
  arithmetic on a latency.
- Per-pass latency is the engine crate's own `infer latency` line. The
  harness's engine `p50_ms` is the engine's detect call over at most the
  run's last minute, the wait for the model lock included: with several
  cameras on one engine it reads several times higher (179–535 ms in the
  boundary cells).

## Why the NPU is half idle at saturation

The probe binary, one stream, 200 passes back to back, p50 ms:

| Rung | Input hand-over | `rknn_run` | Output read | CPU time in the pass |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| tiny 416 | 1.09 | 43.9 | 0.78 | 24.1 |
| tiny 416, again | 1.01 | 39.2 | 0.78 | 19.7 |
| tiny 640 | 2.00 | 79.4 | 1.67 | 35.0 |
| yolox_s 640 | 2.02 | 96.5 | 1.67 | 34.9 |

The CPU column is the calling thread's CPU time across the whole pass:
between a third and just over half of the pass's duration. Where in the
pass it falls was not measured. It is consistent with one context leaving
the NPU 40–51 % busy at saturation, and with a second context adding
throughput (below).

probe) in two and a half hours.

## Not measured

- No cell ran above 2 fps. The loader gives a fleet of one or two cameras
  a higher rate from the same budget: up to 7.5 passes/s on yolox_s, where
  one saturated context carried 7.64–7.92.
- More than one context is not a boundary run, and the shipped engine
  cannot hold one.
- Nothing here ran longer than 300 s.
